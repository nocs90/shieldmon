# 0014 — VEX: scoped not_affected statements, OpenVEX and CycloneDX

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-27

## Context

- A `not_affected` decision is recorded on one finding (ADR 0004, ADR 0010). VEX statements
  come from vendors and other teams and speak about a product, a package and a
  vulnerability: "CVE-X does not affect image Y" must also hold for findings detected after
  the statement arrives, and for the next build of the same repository if the statement
  says so.
- OpenVEX 0.2.0 is the exchange format F5 names; CycloneDX VEX is what CycloneDX-based
  tooling emits. Both are hostile input (I7).
- The OpenVEX schema forbids properties it does not define, so nothing ShieldMon-specific can
  be added to a statement. It has no expiry.
- Closure needs evidence or a decision with author and justification (I4); history is
  append-only (I5).

## Decision

1. **Statements are scoped decisions.** `vex_statements` is append-only and holds, per
   statement: vulnerability (canonical ID and aliases), product (an image digest, or a
   repository for every digest of it), optional package (a PURL with version matches that
   version, without version every version), justification, impact, author, timestamp,
   optional expiry, and the document's `@id`. Only `not_affected` is stored: `affected`,
   `fixed` and `under_investigation` say nothing ShieldMon should act on, and are reported.
2. **Applied through decisions.** A statement in force becomes a `not_affected` decision on
   each matching active finding through `store.ApplyDecision` (same policy, author =
   statement author, reason names the statement). `vex import` applies statements at once;
   the engine applies statements in force after every reconcile, so new findings are covered
   too. A statement applies once per finding (`vex_applications`, append-only): a finding
   the engine reopens later (a regression, an expired decision) is not suppressed again by
   the same statement without anyone noticing. Like any decision (ADR 0004), it follows the
   problem to the successor digest, and keeps the statement's id (`decisions.vex_statement_id`)
   there. A statement whose product has never been scanned is kept and reported.
3. **Import.** `shieldmon vex import FILE` detects OpenVEX or CycloneDX VEX. Every statement
   needs an author (the document's, or `--author`) and a justification (OpenVEX
   `justification` or `impact_statement`; CycloneDX `analysis.justification` or `detail`,
   mapped to OpenVEX justifications). Statements whose expiry has passed are refused.
   Documents are bounded (16 MiB, 100 000 statements, field lengths) and parsers are fuzzed.
4. **Expiry and author in `status_notes`.** An OpenVEX statement has no expiry and no author
   of its own (the document has one), and the schema allows no extra property. ShieldMon
   writes and reads them as lines of `status_notes`: `expires: <RFC 3339>`, and
   `author: <name>` when it is not the document's. Any other text there is kept as is.
5. **Export.** `shieldmon vex export` emits one OpenVEX 0.2.0 document with the statements in
   force and the `not_affected` decisions taken by hand (as digest- and PURL-scoped
   statements). Products are `pkg:oci` PURLs with the digest as version and
   `repository_url`. Risk acceptances are not exported: they say "affected, accepted until",
   which a VEX consumer must not read as not_affected. Tests validate exports against the
   OpenVEX schema (pinned in testdata) and check that import → export → import is lossless.

## Consequences

- A vendor's "not affected" survives rebuilds when scoped to the repository, and is still
  one statement to review or let expire.
- A statement ends at its expiry; there is no manual way to undo a not_affected decision
  before it, as for decisions taken by hand (ADR 0010). Importing a statement is therefore a
  reviewed step: `vex import --dry-run` shows what it would record.
- CycloneDX VEX is imported, not exported; OpenVEX is the export format.
